# Tracks and Value Chain

Do not confuse segmentation axes with value-chain stages. Tracks describe how the market is classified; the value chain describes how products, services, money, data, and bargaining power move.

## Workflow

1. Inherit the approved scope contract without modification.
2. Choose non-overlapping segmentation axes: product, technology, application, customer, geography, business model, or price tier.
3. For each axis, define nodes, allocation rules, overlaps, and unknown buckets.
4. Map upstream, midstream, downstream, supporting infrastructure, channels, users, and payers.
5. Describe material, service, data, cash, and influence flows.
6. Identify value capture, cost concentration, switching costs, control points, bottlenecks, and dependencies.
7. Cross-check that track totals and chain scope remain compatible with sizing and competition modules.

## Hard failures

- Treating a value-chain stage as if it were a mutually exclusive market segment.
- Omitting the payer, user, channel, or value-capture mechanism.
- Mixing core and adjacent markets without disclosure.
- Presenting decorative diagrams with no allocation or flow logic.

## Output contract

Return segmentation trees, allocation notes, a value-chain map, actor roles, economic flows, bottlenecks, evidence IDs, and unresolved issues.
