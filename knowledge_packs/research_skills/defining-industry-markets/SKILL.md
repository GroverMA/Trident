# Industry Definition

Use this method before search, sizing, competition, drivers, forecasting, or reporting. Treat the approved definition as a scope contract; downstream modules must not silently change it.

## Workflow

1. Parse the user's object, geography, period, customer, application, technology, value-chain, and metric intent.
2. Produce D0-D7: plain-language definition, product/service boundary, customer/application boundary, geography, time boundary, value-chain boundary, statistical unit, and named adjacent markets.
3. State inclusions and exclusions as testable rules, not examples only.
4. Separate core market, optional extensions, adjacent markets, substitutes, and out-of-scope activities.
5. Identify unresolved ambiguities and ask for a researcher ruling at Gate 0.
6. Lock the confirmed definition and propagate it to every later module.

## Evidence and writing rules

- Prefer regulator, statistical agency, standards body, industry association, and company primary sources for taxonomy.
- Preserve the user's wording while distinguishing it from the analyst's interpretation.
- Never invent a statistical definition merely because a commercial report uses the same market name.
- Explain how revenue, shipment, installed base, company, or other units map to the boundary.
- Cite evidence for externally asserted boundaries and label analyst judgments.

## Hard failures

- No explicit inclusions and exclusions.
- Geography, period, value-chain position, or statistical unit is missing.
- A downstream module changes scope without returning to Gate 0.
- Adjacent or substitute markets are counted as core without a stated rule.

## Output contract

Return a scope contract, unresolved questions, researcher confirmations, evidence IDs, and a versioned methodology trace.
