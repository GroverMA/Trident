# Competitive Landscape

Competitive claims must refer to the approved market and a comparable business perimeter. Company-wide revenue is not automatically market revenue.

## Workflow

1. Build the company universe from multiple source types and explain coverage limits.
2. Map each company to the target business, products, geography, customers, channel, and value-chain role.
3. Normalize year, region, business scope, currency, and metric before ranking or share calculation.
4. Separate direct competitors, substitutes, adjacent players, entrants, suppliers, and partners.
5. Assess positioning, product/technology differentiation, route to market, economics, capabilities, and strategic moves.
6. Calculate concentration or market share only when numerator and denominator are genuinely comparable.
7. Downgrade unsupported rankings to directional tiers or qualitative positioning.

## Evidence rules

- Prefer filings, company reports, regulator records, tender data, and authoritative databases.
- Label company statements, third-party estimates, and analyst inferences separately.
- Every material comparison needs a source and a comparability note.

## Hard failures

- Mixing global company revenue with regional market size.
- Ranking companies using different years or business scopes without adjustment.
- Claiming market share without a compatible denominator.
- Omitting important players solely because public data are sparse.

## Output contract

Return company universe, business mapping, comparability matrix, competitive groups, positioning, concentration assessment, strategic moves, evidence IDs, and limitations.
